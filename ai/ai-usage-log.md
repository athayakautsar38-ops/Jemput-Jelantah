# AI Usage Log

## 1. Purpose

This document records how AI was used throughout the Jemput Jelantah product development process.

AI was used as an **execution and reasoning accelerator**, while product decisions, evidence interpretation, implementation review, and final validation remained human responsibilities.

The working principle is:

> **AI can accelerate the work. Human judgment validates the work.**

---

# 2. AI-Assisted Product Development Model

The project followed this workflow:

```text
Product Question
      ↓
Human Context & Evidence
      ↓
AI-Assisted Analysis
      ↓
Human Review
      ↓
Product Decision
      ↓
Implementation
      ↓
Testing
      ↓
Iteration
```

AI outputs were treated as working material rather than automatically accepted decisions.

# 5. Research Analysis

AI was used to organize and interpret the available research data.

Current primary research consisted of:

* 15 survey respondents
* 3 user interviews

  * 2 household users
  * 1 culinary micro-UMKM user

AI-assisted activities included:

* grouping recurring pain points,
* identifying patterns across responses,
* organizing qualitative findings,
* converting observations into potential insights,
* identifying areas requiring further validation.

### Evidence control

AI was explicitly instructed not to:

* fabricate respondents,
* invent interview quotes,
* create unsupported percentages,
* treat assumptions as findings,
* present dummy operational data as real evidence.

Research conclusions were checked against the original survey and interview evidence.

# 16. AI Output Review

AI-generated outputs were reviewed using the following checklist:

### Product accuracy

* Does the output match the actual product?
* Does it match the current MVP?
* Does it introduce unsupported functionality?

### Evidence integrity

* Is the claim supported by actual evidence?
* Is the data source known?
* Is the result based on research, assumption, demo data, or future validation?

### Business logic

* Does the proposed logic match the intended operating model?
* Could the implementation create an incorrect transaction or metric?

### Technical feasibility

* Can the proposed solution be implemented within the current stack?
* Does it introduce unnecessary complexity?

### User experience

* Does the solution reduce user friction?
* Does it make important states understandable?

### Scope

* Is the proposed feature part of the MVP?
* Does it introduce unnecessary scope?

# 17. Evidence Classification

During AI-assisted analysis, information was classified into four categories:

| Classification         | Meaning                                                       |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Research Evidence**  | Directly supported by the 15 survey responses or 3 interviews |
| **Product Assumption** | Working assumption used to design or test the MVP             |
| **Demo Data**          | Seeded/illustrative data used to demonstrate functionality    |
| **Validated Result**   | Outcome supported by actual execution and measurement         |

This distinction was particularly important when AI generated numerical examples or operational scenarios.

---

# 18. Dummy Data Control

The MVP contains illustrative/demo records to demonstrate product functionality.

Examples may include:

* supplier counts,
* pickup requests,
* route volumes,
* completed pickups,
* pricing references,
* dashboard metrics.

These records are **not treated as actual traction or validated business outcomes** unless they are subsequently replaced or confirmed by real operational data.

The rule is:

```text
Demo Data
≠
Research Evidence
≠
Operational Evidence
≠
Validated Business Outcome
```

---

# 19. What AI Was Not Used For

AI was not treated as an authoritative source for:

* user research evidence,
* market validation,
* actual customer behavior,
* actual operational performance,
* actual business economics,
* real-world UCO pricing without a verified source,
* claims of product-market fit,
* claims of traction.

AI-generated information requiring external factual verification was treated as an input for further checking, not as evidence by default.

# 23. Final Principle

The purpose of using AI in Jemput Jelantah was not to automate product judgment.

It was to reduce execution time, broaden exploration, identify potential blind spots, and accelerate iteration.

The operating principle throughout the project is:

> **AI-assisted, human-validated product development.**

The quality of the product is therefore evaluated not by how much AI-generated output was produced, but by how effectively AI-assisted work was converted into **evidence-backed product decisions and validated implementation**.
