# Research

## 1. Research Objective

The research was conducted to understand how households and culinary micro-UMKM currently manage used cooking oil (UCO), identify the main barriers to collection, and determine what conditions could support a scheduled pickup model.

The research focused on four questions:

1. How do potential suppliers currently manage UCO?
2. What are the main barriers to selling or handing over UCO?
3. What factors would make a pickup service useful and trustworthy?
4. How should these findings influence the initial target market and product design?

---

## 2. Research Method

The research used a combination of qualitative interviews and a quantitative survey.

### 2.1 User Interviews

Three exploratory interviews were conducted:

* 2 household users
* 1 culinary micro-UMKM operator

The interviews explored:

* UCO generation and storage habits
* Current disposal or selling behavior
* Difficulties in finding collection channels
* Perception of UCO selling price
* Interest in pickup services
* Expectations around weighing and payment

The interviews were used to identify recurring themes and generate product hypotheses.

### 2.2 User Survey

A Google Form survey was conducted with 15 respondents:

* 10 households
* 5 culinary micro-UMKM

The survey covered:

* Estimated weekly UCO generation
* Current UCO handling behavior
* Main UCO management challenges
* Interest in pickup services
* Important service attributes
* Willingness to use scheduled area-based pickup

---

## 3. Survey Findings

### 3.1 Interest in Pickup

All 15 respondents rated their interest in a direct pickup service at 4 or 5 out of 5.

**Result: 15/15 (100%)**

This indicates strong stated interest in a pickup-based collection model within the surveyed sample.

However, stated interest should not be treated as proof of actual adoption. Actual behavior still needs to be validated through the MVP and pilot.

---

### 3.2 Willingness to Use Scheduled Area Pickup

Respondents were asked whether they would use a scheduled pickup model.

* Yes: 11/15 (73.3%)
* Maybe: 4/15 (26.7%)

No respondent selected a negative response.

This supports testing scheduled area-based pickup as part of the initial product model.

---

### 3.3 Access to a Collection Channel

Five of the 15 respondents indicated that they did not know where to sell or hand over their UCO.

**Result: 5/15 (33.3%)**

This suggests that access to a clear collection channel is a relevant problem for a portion of the surveyed users.

---

### 3.4 Price Transparency

Five of the 15 respondents indicated that they did not know a fair selling price for their UCO.

**Result: 5/15 (33.3%)**

Clear selling price was selected as an important service factor by all 15 respondents.

**Result: 15/15 (100%)**

This makes price transparency an important product requirement to validate.

---

### 3.5 Pickup Cost

Nine of the 15 respondents selected "no pickup fee" as an important factor.

**Result: 9/15 (60%)**

This suggests that pickup convenience alone may not be sufficient; perceived cost can also influence the value proposition.

---

### 3.6 Transparent Weighing

Four of the 15 respondents selected transparent weighing as an important factor.

**Result: 4/15 (26.7%)**

Although this was selected less frequently than price transparency, transparent weighing was incorporated into the MVP because actual volume determines supplier payout.

---

## 4. Current UCO Handling

The survey showed several different UCO handling behaviors:

* Some households rarely have leftover oil.
* Some respondents dispose of UCO.
* Some respondents store UCO.
* Some respondents sell UCO to existing collectors.
* Some respondents were unsure where to sell or hand over UCO.

The variation indicates that UCO supply is not only fragmented across users, but also differs in volume and behavior.

---

## 5. UCO Volume Patterns

The survey used broad self-reported weekly volume categories rather than precise measurements.

### Household respondents

Among 10 households:

* Less than 1L: 2 respondents
* 1–3L: 4 respondents
* 4–5L: 0 respondents
* 6–10L: 0 respondents
* More than 10L: 0 respondents
* Did not know: 4 respondents

### Culinary micro-UMKM respondents

Among 5 UMKM:

* Less than 1L: 0 respondents
* 1–3L: 0 respondents
* 4–5L: 1 respondent
* 6–10L: 2 respondents
* More than 10L: 1 respondent
* Did not know: 1 respondent

The sample indicates that culinary micro-UMKM may contribute higher individual volumes than households in the current sample.

This is directional rather than representative because the sample size is small and volume was self-reported.

---

## 6. Interview Synthesis

The three interviews were used to explore the reasons behind the survey patterns.

The research focused on three themes:

### 6.1 Collection Access

Users may have UCO available but do not always have a clear or convenient channel to hand it over.

### 6.2 Volume Fragmentation

Household contributions can be relatively small and inconsistent. Individual household pickups may therefore create operational inefficiency if handled without sufficient geographic density.

### 6.3 Trust in Transaction

Users need clarity around how their UCO volume is measured and how the final payout is calculated.

These themes were used as hypotheses for product design rather than treated as statistically validated findings.

---

## 7. Key Pain Points

Based on the combined research:

### Pain Point 1 — Fragmented Supply

UCO is distributed across many potential suppliers with different contribution volumes.

### Pain Point 2 — Limited Collection Access

A portion of respondents do not know where to sell or hand over their UCO.

### Pain Point 3 — Price Uncertainty

A portion of respondents do not know a fair selling price.

### Pain Point 4 — Operational Density

Small and irregular contributions can make individual pickup routes inefficient if suppliers are geographically dispersed.

### Pain Point 5 — Transaction Transparency

The actual collected volume affects payout, creating a need for transparent weighing and payout calculation.

---

## 8. Root Cause Analysis

The research led to the following working root-cause model:

**Fragmented Supply**

↓

**Uneven Supplier Contribution**

↓

**Insufficient Route Density**

↓

**Risk of Low Route Utilization**

The product opportunity is therefore not simply to create another UCO pickup channel.

The initial product hypothesis is to aggregate nearby suppliers before dispatching a pickup route.
